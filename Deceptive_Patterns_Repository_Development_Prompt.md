# Repository development prompt

Copy the prompt below into an agent that can read GitHub repositories and create local files. It commissions a documentation, research and agent-governance repository, not a production monitoring service. The source links are starting points: the agent must inspect current files and record the versions it actually uses.

## Begin prompt

You are developing a new Double FW Ltd knowledge repository provisionally titled **Deceptive Patterns and Inclusive Experience Evaluation**. Suggested repository slug: `Deceptive-Patterns-and-Inclusive-Experience-Evaluation`.

Create a complete, reviewable first edition using relevant resources in the [Double-FW GitHub account](https://github.com/Double-FW). Deliver the actual files, not just a proposal or a list of future tasks. Use British English and write for accessibility, design, engineering, research, product, governance and operational managers, as well as AI coding and review agents.

### 1 Purpose and intended outcomes

Develop a practical method for identifying, investigating, preventing and remediating deceptive, coercive and exclusionary design and organisational practices. Combine:

1. Established patterns and documented mechanisms of deception, pressure, obstruction and manipulation.
2. Applicable accessibility requirements, technical findings and other relevant compliance evidence.
3. Task performance, satisfaction and qualitative experience evidence, analysed through voluntary functional access-needs segments informed by the EAA Annex I Section VII functional performance criteria.

The objective is informed choice, independent participation and comparable outcomes. Successful task completion alone is insufficient when people incur disproportionate effort, disclose unnecessary personal information, misunderstand a decision, accept an unwanted outcome or depend on someone else.

Cover websites, applications, internal systems, AI interfaces and connected service journeys. Include organisational practices such as support-channel restrictions, repeated evidence demands, inaccessible cancellation, procurement decisions, escalation loops and handoffs. Examine cumulative effects across systems and over time.

Do not optimise conversion, retention, engagement or average satisfaction at the expense of autonomy and access. Do not provide instructions for deploying manipulative practices. Examples must support recognition, investigation and prevention.

### 3 Source limits and external verification

Keep Double-FW resources as the primary methodological foundation. Do not assume they contain a complete established dark-pattern taxonomy.

Where a named established pattern lacks a verified source, record the gap and mark the entry as provisional synthesis. Do not invent citations or label new classifications as established consensus.

Targeted external verification is permitted only to fill a documented gap: use original research or primary institutional publications for established deceptive-pattern classifications, and official legal or standards sources for exact requirements. Begin EAA verification with [Directive EU 2019/882 on EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2019/882/oj). Follow relevant links already supplied by the Double-FW sources. Avoid an unrestricted literature search.

Keep external contributions separately attributed. Record inaccessible or paywalled sources as unavailable; do not reconstruct their contents from memory. If verification is unavailable, complete the remaining repository and mark affected conclusions as unresolved. Do not invent permissions or apply a repository licence to third-party material automatically. Preserve existing licences and flag unresolved licensing decisions.

### 4 Define classifications and evidence boundaries

Provide operational definitions and decision rules for:

- Deceptive or coercive patterns: mechanisms that mislead, pressure, obstruct or undermine informed, freely exercised choice.
- Exclusionary design or practice: barriers producing unequal access, effort, participation or outcomes, whether or not deception is established.
- Accessibility failures: failures against identified requirements within a defined evaluation scope.
- General usability problems: difficulty without sufficient evidence of a deceptive mechanism or discriminatory effect.
- Legitimate complexity or safeguards: necessary demands whose purpose, proportionality, alternatives and outcomes require examination.
- Suspected patterns and unresolved findings: signals requiring investigation.

Allow multiple labels with a rationale. Keep category, evidence status, severity, confidence and legal review status separate. A known pattern match is an investigation lead, not automatic proof that a particular implementation is harmful or unlawful.

Low satisfaction alone does not establish deception. High satisfaction does not invalidate a reproducible access barrier. A compliance failure does not establish intent. A harmful mechanism may be evidenced without proving intent. Do not make allegations about an organisation or individual beyond the evidence.

For every finding, distinguish observation, participant report, inference, alternative explanation and proposed action. Account for context such as device, familiarity, task difficulty, time pressure, environment and access method without using those factors to dismiss exclusion.

### 5 Build the pattern and practice catalogue

Include a source-grounded initial catalogue covering concealed costs or conditions, misleading hierarchy or wording, obstructed refusal or cancellation, unequal effort between choices, forced disclosure or registration, repeated pressure, false urgency or scarcity, unwanted defaults, consent interference, inaccessible support and abandonment of responsibility across handoffs. Add other categories only where evidence supports them.

For each entry provide a stable ID, definition, mechanism, affected decisions, scope, source status, observable indicators, counterexamples, accessibility interactions, relevant functional access lenses, potential harms, investigation methods and accessible alternatives. Include legitimate lookalikes and evidence that would weaken or disconfirm the hypothesis.

Map to a technical or legal requirement only where a verified relationship exists. Use explicit relationship types such as direct requirement, supporting evidence, related consideration or no mapping established. Do not imply that every deceptive practice is a WCAG or EAA failure.

### 6 Use functional performance criteria responsibly

Verify the exact current Annex I Section VII criteria, identifiers, wording and application conditions against an official source. Produce a traceable mapping table. Separate the legal criteria from the research segmentation model derived from them.

Investigate access without vision, limited vision, colour perception, hearing, limited hearing, speech, manipulation or strength, reach, photosensitivity and cognition, alongside privacy when accessibility features are used. Verify the official grouping rather than inventing a new legal taxonomy.

Use voluntary, accessible, purpose-limited questions about functional needs and access approaches. Allow overlapping membership, changing needs and non-disclosure. Do not require diagnosis or infer disability from behaviour, device, assistive technology use or telemetry.

Do not turn privacy or seizure-related requirements into compulsory personal disclosures. Evaluate design conditions directly where appropriate. Explain why functional criteria do not supply a complete model of disability, identity, vulnerability or service context.

Include disabled people in defining questions, shaping the method and evaluating outcomes. Do not defer all participation until final testing. Resolve known technical defects before asking participants to spend time rediscovering them, while retaining early discovery and co-design.

### 7 Design combined evaluation and analysis

Create a repeatable workflow:

1. Establish scope, service purpose, user goals, requirements, business incentives and owners.
2. Map complete journeys, alternative channels and cumulative barriers.
3. Review potential pattern mechanisms and select relevant technical tests.
4. Gather proportionate task, qualitative and experience evidence using an approved research protocol.
5. Analyse results by task, access needs, access method, environment and relevant intersections.
6. Triangulate findings and test alternative explanations.
7. Prioritise action, implement changes and verify both access and informed choice.
8. Monitor recurrence and update shared patterns, policy and training.

Measure completion and correctness, understanding of consequences, time and effort, errors and recovery, assistance, unwanted acceptance, abandonment, autonomy, privacy, confidence, trust and satisfaction. Define each measure, denominator, context and limitations. Include people who fail, abandon or cannot reach a survey; satisfaction from successful completers alone is biased.

Compare experiences of accepting, refusing, cancelling, correcting and recovering. Do not assume all tasks have equally legitimate symmetry requirements: justify necessary safeguards and assess accessible alternatives. Capture delayed harms and cumulative burden as well as immediate reactions.

Provide accessible neutral survey questions and interview prompts. Keep objective outcomes separate from perceptions. Report sample sizes, missingness, non-response, uncertainty, overlapping groups and selection effects. Do not invent universal minimum sample sizes or significance thresholds. Do not infer causation from group differences alone, average away serious minority findings or treat non-significance as equivalence.

Provide privacy-preserving reporting rules for small cells and intersections, with project-specific disclosure-risk review. Keep participant-level data outside the public repository. Include consent, access controls, retention, deletion and escalation rules without inventing a universal retention period.

Ethical evaluation must not knowingly expose participants to harmful treatment merely to obtain an A/B result. Use proportionate simulations, moderated tasks, prior evidence or staged remediation where appropriate. Never treat synthetic personas or generated survey responses as research evidence.

### 8 Make automation boundaries explicit

For each test identify whether it is automated, assisted, expert review, research with participants or operational review. Record prerequisites, steps, expected outcome, evidence and limitations.

Agents may inspect wording and interface structure, trace journeys, flag candidate mechanisms, run configured accessibility checks, reconcile approved datasets, draft findings and coordinate authorised remediation. They must report coverage and uncertainty.

Agents must not claim to have established intent, legal breach, consent validity or equitable experience from a screenshot, DOM inspection, automated pass or satisfaction score alone. Never infer disability or deploy individual vulnerability scoring. Do not invent a comprehensive detector or claim supported integrations that have not been built and verified.

Define authorised autonomy, human owners, escalation triggers, stop conditions and recovery. Require audit records for escalations with necessary evidence references, reason, owner, decision and follow-up. Minimise personal data; configure retention and redaction through approved policy. Do not put credentials or participant identities in public logs.
